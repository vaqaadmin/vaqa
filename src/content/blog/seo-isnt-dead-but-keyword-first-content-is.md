SEO isn't dead. Keyword-first reporting is, and most agencies haven't noticed yet.

## Key takeaways

- Ranking reports measure a search behaviour that's shrinking every quarter
- Answer engine visibility is now the metric that predicts pipeline
- Founders should ask agencies how they show up in AI answers, not Google
- Traditional SEO skills still matter, but the scoreboard has changed
- GEO and AEO aren't buzzwords, they're the new distribution layer

I've spent the last few years building FirstMotion around exactly this shift, working with B2B SaaS companies who were still being sold on keyword volume while their buyers had already moved to asking ChatGPT and Perplexity for recommendations. Before that, at Obby and Baluu, I watched organic acquisition channels change shape more than once, and the pattern is always the same: the tools change faster than the reporting does. This piece is about why I think keyword-first reporting is actively misleading founders right now, and what to track instead.

## Why keyword rankings stopped telling the truth

A ranking report tells you where a page sits on a results page that fewer and fewer people are scrolling through. Google itself has leaned hard into AI Overviews, and tools like [ChatGPT](https://openai.com), [Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai), and [Anthropic's Claude](https://www.anthropic.com) have become genuine research assistants for B2B buyers doing vendor comparisons.

I'm not saying nobody clicks a blue link anymore. I'm saying the click is no longer the only, or even the primary, moment where a brand gets chosen. A lot of purchase consideration now happens inside a chat window, and if your brand isn't part of the answer, you never make the shortlist.

That's the part keyword rank trackers can't see. They were built for a world where the search results page was the whole game.

## What answer engine visibility actually measures

Answer engine optimisation, or GEO if you prefer that label, is about whether your content gets pulled into the answer itself. That means three things happening at once.

1. The model has to know your brand exists and what it does.
2. Your content has to be structured so it's easy to extract and cite.
3. Third-party sources have to reinforce your claims, because models weight corroboration heavily.

None of that shows up in a rank tracker. It shows up in citation frequency, in brand mention rate across AI answers, and in whether your product gets recommended unprompted when someone asks a comparison question. Those are the numbers I ask clients to start pulling before anything else.

## The reporting gap that's costing founders budget

Here's the uncomfortable bit. A lot of agencies are still billing for keyword-first reporting because it's easy to produce and it's what clients expect to see.

I think that's a disservice. A founder looking at a dashboard full of green arrows next to keyword positions can feel like the SEO programme is working, while their actual share of voice in AI answers is quietly eroding to a competitor who invested in structured, citable content.

The fix isn't complicated, but it does require a different reporting rhythm:

| Old metric | What it misses | Better replacement |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword position | Doesn't capture AI answer inclusion | Brand mention rate in AI answers |
| Organic sessions | Blends AI referral traffic with search | Referral source segmentation including AI platforms |
| Backlink count | Volume without authority weighting | Citation quality from sources models trust |
| Page 1 rankings | Ignores zero-click and AI-summarised queries | Share of voice against named competitors |

I'd rather a client see a slightly less flattering report that reflects reality than a vanity report that reflects nothing.

## What actually moves answer engine visibility

Content structure matters more than it used to. Clear, direct answers near the top of a page, genuine expertise signals, and a site architecture that makes it easy for a crawler to understand what you actually do all help.

So does being mentioned elsewhere. Digital PR, credible third-party coverage, and community presence on platforms like Reddit and G2 all feed into how confidently a model will cite you. I've seen this first hand running GEO programmes at FirstMotion: the brands that show up consistently in AI answers are rarely the ones with the most backlinks. They're the ones that are talked about accurately, consistently, and in multiple places.

There's also a technical layer that's easy to overlook. Making sure your site is actually crawlable by AI bots, that your schema markup is clean, and that your key claims are stated plainly rather than buried in marketing language, all increase the odds of being extracted correctly.

## Why founders should still care about traditional SEO

I want to be clear about something, because I think this gets misread. I'm not telling founders to abandon SEO fundamentals. Technical health, site speed, and genuinely useful content are still the foundation everything else sits on.

What I am saying is that the reporting layer built on top of that foundation needs to change. Keyword rank is a leading indicator for a channel that's shrinking in relative importance. Answer engine visibility is the leading indicator for where buyer attention is actually going.

## The reporting shift I'd make this quarter

If I were running marketing for a B2B SaaS business right now, I'd ask my team or agency one question: can you show me our visibility inside AI answers for the queries our buyers actually use. If the honest answer is no, that's the gap to close first.

This isn't a hypothetical for me. It's the whole reason FirstMotion exists as an agency, and it's a conversation I have constantly in advisory sessions with founders who are still budgeting for last decade's SEO scoreboard.

Reporting on rankings alone isn't wrong because rankings don't matter at all. It's wrong because it answers a question buyers have largely stopped asking.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is traditional SEO actually dead?

No, and I think that framing is lazy. Technical SEO, site structure, and genuinely useful content still matter enormously; what's changed is that keyword ranking alone is no longer a sufficient measure of whether that work is paying off.

### What is GEO or AEO in simple terms?

GEO, generative engine optimisation, and AEO, answer engine optimisation, both describe the practice of optimising content so AI tools like ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews are more likely to cite or recommend your brand. It's the natural evolution of SEO for a world where answers, not just links, are the output.

### How do I actually measure answer engine visibility?

Look at how often your brand is mentioned or cited across AI platforms for queries relevant to your category, ideally benchmarked against named competitors. This is exactly the kind of tracking FirstMotion builds for clients, because off-the-shelf rank trackers weren't built to capture it.
